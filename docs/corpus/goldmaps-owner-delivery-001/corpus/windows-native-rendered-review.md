## Blocking gallery defects

The default fitted 992 × 656 gallery, explicit 1280 × 720 gallery and 1380 × 880 gallery all show the same layout problems: the navigation row consumes most or all left-column height, Previous/Next buttons become very tall, and the keyboard hint wraps one character per line. A tiny unintended “Your l…” popup overlays the page. The largest image exposes only a shallow map-list strip; the smaller images expose no usable list area. These are actual visual failures even though capture generation and native identity checks passed. Specialist B and the lead were notified immediately.

A source-corrected layout must be rendered and independently reviewed before browser readiness is claimed. The original images remain negative evidence; they must not be silently replaced in the predecessor qualification. The exact three gallery PNGs, their result receipts and graphical summary are retained in [the existing negative-evidence archive](../qualification/windows-native-gallery-defect.v1.zip) (`439,628` bytes; SHA-256 `38eda5f8a031cbc9a2d3e569c1bf48ffa5df642fa27593ed885cc2895f75d42f`). Its eleven-member ZIP passed CRC verification, and all seven graphical members were independently byte-compared with the downloaded originals. This small evidence archive is separate from the actual playable evaluator.

## Limits and next gate

The ordinary Workbench controls and fine portrait details are small at the captured resolution. Full-resolution preview, zoom/focus and owner physical testing remain necessary for detailed readability and high-speed judgement. B3 stays inconclusive, D1 keeps its assessor disagreement, and D2 retains the historical prior-reference preference without adoption. All owner decisions remain pending.

The predecessor initial gallery image repeats Gold197 before selecting each requested native map. No D1 comparison dialog, Gold125 decision dialog or Gold197 overview popup is captured here. Those targeted views and the corrected lifecycle belong to the later product source and must be reported separately.
